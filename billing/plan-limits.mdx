Every plan includes an allowance across three things: how many Links you can hold, how many editors can sign in to manage them, and how many numbered sequence patterns you can claim. All three increase as you move up the tiers.

| | Starter | Professional | Scale |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Links** | 100 | 1,000 | 10,000 |
| **Editors** | 1 | 5 | 20 |
| **Numbered sequence patterns** | — | 1 | 5 |
| **Scanning** | Public | Public | Public or private |

[qrtub.com/pricing](https://qrtub.com/pricing) is the current source for these figures and for prices. Enterprise is arranged directly and is not fixed to the numbers above.

Your own team's allowances, and how much of each you have used, are on the team page under **Subscription**, in the **Plan usage** panel: one bar each for **Links**, **Editors** and **Numbered patterns**, read as "used of allowed". An allowance your plan does not include reads **Not included in this plan**. Only the team owner sees it.

## What counts as a Link

Every Link you hold, whether or not it is connected to anything yet.

That matters more than it sounds, because of how QRtub is normally used. A Link is a permanent address, and producing Tags before you know what they will point to is a deliberate workflow — so a batch of 500 Tags sitting in a box, connected to nothing, is 500 Links against your allowance. Spare codes count too. The allowance is a count of addresses you have brought into existence, not a count of the ones currently in use.

It is also a total, not a monthly rate. You can create your whole allowance on your first day — which is the point, since setting up a production run is an afternoon's work rather than something you meter out over a year.

## What counts as an editor

People who sign in to manage your Links: creating Links, updating destinations, editing Pages, connecting codes to Items.

In practice it is a count of seats on the team. Every member takes one, the owner included, and so does every invitation that has been sent but not yet accepted or expired. Starter's single seat is the owner's, so inviting anyone needs Professional or Scale.

**People who scan your codes are not editors and never need an account.** Public scanning is unlimited and free on every tier. You are paying for the people running the system, not the people using it — a council with five staff and forty thousand residents scanning bin codes needs five editors.

The Owner and Editor role labels inside a team are a different question — those are about what a member is allowed to do, not about what your plan includes. See [Team Roles](/team/roles).

## What counts as a numbered sequence pattern

A claimed range of sequential Links — `CRA0001TL` through `CRA0999TL`, for example.

This is the number people misread, because a pattern is a reserved *range* rather than a single code. One pattern can cover hundreds of Links, which is why the allowance is a much smaller figure than the other two. Numbered patterns start at Professional; Starter does not include them. That includes building a Collection's Links from Item IDs, which uses a numbered pattern. On a plan without them, the **Numbered** option and the ID-based link setting show an upgrade panel instead of the pattern fields. A team that already holds a pattern from a bigger plan keeps using it.

The Links minted inside a pattern still count against your Link allowance in the normal way. Claiming the range itself does not consume Links — only the codes you actually mint from it do. See [Numbered Links](/links/numbered-links).

## Near or at a limit

QRtub tells you before a request would go over, beside the control that spends the allowance. An amber note appears when you have used 80% or more of an allowance, or when the thing you are about to create will not fit: for example "12 of 100 links left. This needs 40." The owner gets a way to upgrade (**See plans**, or **Change your plan** on a team that is already paying); other members are told to ask the team owner. You will see it on the Create Link form, the invite form, the CSV import previews for Links and Items, the Item form when saving a new Item creates a Link, and a Collection's Item ID pattern.

The note never disables anything, and an action that spends nothing (editing an existing Item, reusing a numbered pattern you already own) shows no note at all. The limit itself is enforced when you save: a request that would take the team over is refused with a message naming the limit, such as "This team has used every link its plan includes. Upgrade, or delete some links to free up room."

If a team is already over an allowance, for example after moving to a smaller plan, nothing it already has is removed and its codes keep working. Only new Links, seats or patterns are refused until it is back under the limit.

## What is the same on every plan

Scanning is never limited or charged. Neither is changing where an existing Link points — updating a destination without remaking the Tag is included on every tier, including Starter, because it is the thing QRtub exists to do.
