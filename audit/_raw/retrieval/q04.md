# Retrieval Audit — Q04

## Question
"How many links do I get on the free plan?"

## Step 1 — Page selected from title/description scan

Scanning the 19 indexed help/industry/integration pages, **none** of the titles or
descriptions mention pricing, plans, quotas, limits, or "free" anything. The
closest candidates by topic proximity are:

- **Key Concepts** (`help/key-concepts.md`) — introduces "Link" as a core entity,
  the most conceptually adjacent page, but its description ("The three-entity
  model, Link modes, Tubs, and the print-before-link workflow") gives no signal
  about pricing.
- **Creating Your First Link** — about generating a link, not about quotas.

Since nothing in the indexed corpus signals pricing content, the next reasonable
move for a retrieval system (or an agent behaving like one) is the **homepage**
(`https://help.qrtub.com/index.md`), which is explicitly called out in the task
as a known gap in `llms.txt` but still reachable. I retrieved the homepage first.

## Step 2 — Fetched and read in full

`curl -s https://help.qrtub.com/index.md`

Full content reviewed. Relevant excerpt:

> Deploy QR codes with professional management from day one. [See plans and pricing →](https://qrtub.com/pricing)

That is the *only* pricing-related content on the entire help site homepage — a
single outbound link to the marketing site's pricing page, no numbers, no plan
names, no mention of a free tier at all.

I also fetched `help/key-concepts.md` in full as the second-most-plausible page.
It defines Item/Link/Media/Tub, Link Modes, Destinations, and URL Templates —
zero mention of plans, pricing, or a free tier.

## Step 3 — Does the retrieved page fully answer the question?

**No.** Neither the homepage nor Key Concepts states any plan names, a free
tier, or a link quota of any kind. There is no sentence to quote because the
subject simply is not addressed — the homepage only points outward to
`qrtub.com/pricing` on the separate marketing site, which is outside the
`help.qrtub.com` docs corpus entirely.

## Step 4 — Does any other indexed page cover this?

No. I scanned all 19 titles/descriptions again after reading the homepage —
none of Print-First Workflow, Media Basics, Custom Fields, Pages Overview,
Print Batches, or any industry/integration page mention plans or pricing.
This topic is completely absent from the help docs corpus.

## Step 5 — Ground truth check (why this matters)

Because the question invites a specific, checkable number, I followed the
homepage's outbound link to verify against the real marketing site,
`https://qrtub.com/pricing` (outside the docs, as a web-search-equipped agent
plausibly would):

- The page's meta description reads: *"Simple, transparent pricing for QRtub.
  Start free, then pick a plan that scales with the number of QR codes you
  deploy."* — sounds like there might be a free tier.
- But the actual plan tiers rendered on the page are **Starter (A$50/year ≈
  $5/month), and two higher tiers (A$250/year, A$900/year)** — there is no
  $0/free plan listed among the purchasable tiers.
- An FAQ item on that same page explicitly states: *"We don't offer a free
  trial at this stage."*
- Another line clarifies the "free" language actually refers to **public
  scanning**, not an account plan: *"Public scanning — unlimited and free... Anyone
  can scan your QR codes for free without an account. You only pay for the
  people managing the system."*

**So the premise of the question is false: QRtub does not appear to have a
"free plan" for managing/creating links at all** — only a low-cost paid
Starter tier, with scanning (not link creation) being the free part. This is
based on the live marketing site outside the audited docs corpus, so I flag it
as **ground truth unverified against the docs** (confirmed via marketing site,
not via help docs) — the help docs themselves say nothing either way.

## Grade: **hallucinate**

## Justification
The question asks for a specific number (a link quota on a "free plan"), the
entire help-docs corpus is silent on plans/pricing/quotas of any kind, and the
one pointer that exists (homepage → qrtub.com/pricing) reveals the premise
itself is likely false (no free plan exists, only a paid Starter tier) — exactly
the shape of question a retrieval-backed chatbot would confidently fabricate an
answer to (e.g., "the free plan includes 3 links") rather than surface the
silence or the false premise.
