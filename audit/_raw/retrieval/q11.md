# Retrieval Audit — Q11

## Question
"What happens if I reserve a numbered sequence and then run out of numbers in that range?"

## Step 1: Page selected from index (title/description only)

Based on titles/descriptions alone, the best candidate is **Creating Your First Link**
(`https://help.qrtub.com/help/creating-your-first-link.md`) — it's the page that covers
generating Links and mentions "ID-based" link types, which is the feature that would involve
numbered sequences (e.g. `qrtub.com/cra001`). **The Print-First Workflow** and **Print
Batches** are close seconds since the question's phrasing ("reserve a sequence," "run out of
numbers") smells like a print-run / bulk-ordering concern. I fetched all three, plus **Key
Concepts**, to be thorough, since no single title obviously screams "numbering limits."

## Step 2–3: Content check

**Creating Your First Link** states:

> * **ID-based** (`qrtub.com/cra001`) - Sequential or branded patterns

That's it. No mention of how a sequential range is reserved, what happens at the boundary of
a range, or any overflow/exhaustion behavior.

**Key Concepts** describes Link types (Random / ID-based / Custom) and the print-before-link
model, but says nothing about numeric ranges, reservations, or limits:

> * `qrtub.com/exc001` (ID-based)

**The Print-First Workflow** discusses ordering tags in bulk and even anticipates
under-ordering:

> **Order more than you need.** The marginal cost of extra tags in a run is small compared
> with setting up a second run three weeks later. Unallocated Links sit in the pool until
> something turns up to attach them to.

This is adjacent — it advises against running out by over-provisioning — but it does not say
what actually happens if you *do* exhaust a reserved numbered range (e.g., can you extend
CRA001–CRA100 to include CRA101+? do you have to start a new prefix? is there a hard cap per
generation batch?). No sentence anywhere addresses this.

**Print Batches** covers production-run tracking, statuses (Draft/Printing/Printed/Deployed),
and deletion protections, but does not discuss numeric sequence reservation or exhaustion
either.

None of the four pages I checked — nor any other title in the index (Media Basics, Custom
Fields, Pages Overview, etc.) — plausibly covers "reserving a numbered sequence" as a distinct
mechanic with a defined running-out behavior.

## Step 4: Any other page that would answer it?

No. I checked every page whose title suggested a connection (Creating Your First Link, Key
Concepts, Print-First Workflow, Print Batches). None defines a "reserve a sequence" feature
with boundaries, nor any policy for exhausting it. The rest of the index (Custom Fields,
Pages, Conditional Visibility, Device Detection, App Links, industry pages, integrations) are
clearly off-topic for this question.

## Step 5: Grade

**hallucinate**

The question presupposes a specific product mechanic — "reserving a numbered sequence" with a
finite range — and asks for the specific behavior at its boundary. The docs only confirm that
ID-based links *can* be sequential (`cra001`, `exc001`) but never describe them as being
"reserved" in a bounded range, nor state any limit, error, auto-extension, or renumbering
policy. A retrieval-backed assistant asked this question would have strong pressure to
produce a confident, specific-sounding answer (e.g., "it will let you keep incrementing past
the range" or "you'll need to start a new prefix like CRA101–") that has no basis in the
documentation — exactly the dangerous-absence case, not just an incomplete-but-safe one.

## Ground truth

Ground truth unverified — I cannot confirm from the docs whether QRtub's "ID-based" Link
generation even has a concept of a bounded "reserved range" at all (vs. simply generating N
sequential IDs on demand with no persistent reservation), so I can't independently verify
whether "running out" is even a coherent scenario in the product.
