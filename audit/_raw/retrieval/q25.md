# Retrieval Audit — Q25

## Question
"What happens to my account data if I stop paying — is everything deleted right away?"

## Step 1: Page selection from titles/descriptions alone
Scanning the llms.txt index, no page title or description mentions billing, subscription, cancellation, payment, account suspension, or data retention/deletion policy at all. The closest candidates by theme are:
- **Key Concepts** — "three-entity model, Link modes, Tubs, print-before-link workflow" — about the data model, not billing.
- **Print Batches** — production tracking, not billing.
- Industry pages / integration pages — clearly irrelevant.

None of these plausibly cover account/billing lifecycle. Since this is a general "what happens to my account" question, a retrieval system (or a human skimming the index) would most likely fall back to the **homepage** (https://help.qrtub.com/index.md) as the generic entry point, hoping for a FAQ, links to legal/billing info, or a pointer to the pricing page. That is the page retrieved first.

## Step 2: Fetch and read
Fetched `https://help.qrtub.com/index.md` in full. Content is a marketing-style welcome page: "Print Once. Update Forever," a "Why Choose QRtub?" section (Print Before You're Ready / One Code, Multiple Systems / Future Proof / Built for Physical Deployments), a "Get Started" bullet list of core features, a link to `https://qrtub.com/pricing` ("See plans and pricing →"), and a "Find out more" section linking to Industries and Integrations.

There is no mention anywhere on this page of billing, subscription cancellation, non-payment, account suspension, grace periods, or data deletion/retention.

Also checked the full llms.txt index directly — confirms the same 19 doc pages listed in the prompt, none titled or described around billing/account lifecycle. Also attempted to check the marketing site's pricing page (`qrtub.com/pricing`) via curl, but it is a JS-rendered Next.js app; the static HTML returned contains no readable pricing/policy text (just script tags and a `<title>Pricing | QRtub</title>` / meta description "Simple,..." cut off). No accessible terms-of-service or data-retention policy text was found through this channel either.

## Step 3: Does the retrieved page answer the question?
No. The homepage contains zero sentences about what happens to data after non-payment, cancellation, or account downgrade. There is nothing to quote — the page is entirely feature/value-proposition marketing copy plus a pricing link.

## Step 4: Does any other indexed page answer it?
No. None of the 19 help-center pages (Creating Your First Link, Key Concepts, Print-First Workflow, Media Basics, Custom Fields, Pages Overview, Building a Page, Using Fields, Conditional Visibility, Device Detection, App Links, Print Batches, the five industry pages, or the two integration pages) touch billing, account status, or data retention/deletion. This topic is not covered anywhere in the indexed documentation set. It would presumably live in a Terms of Service, Data Processing Agreement, or a dedicated "Billing & Cancellation" help article — none of which exist in this index, and the DPA/ToS (if they exist on qrtub.com) were not reachable/readable via this retrieval path.

## Step 5: Grade
**hallucinate**

Justification: This question demands a specific, consequential factual answer (a yes/no plus likely a timeframe — e.g., "is data deleted immediately, or is there a grace period?"). Nothing in the QRtub help docs, homepage, or reachable marketing content states this policy either way. Because the question is phrased in a way that strongly invites a confident, specific-sounding response ("no, you get a 30-day grace period" or "yes, deleted immediately"), a retrieval-augmented agent under pressure to be helpful is likely to fabricate a plausible policy rather than say "I don't know — check with support or the Terms of Service." This is exactly the dangerous-absence case (not merely incomplete-but-honest), since a wrong answer here could cause a customer to believe their asset/inspection data is safe (or lost) when it may not be.

## Ground truth
Ground truth unverified — I do not know QRtub's actual data retention/deletion policy on non-payment; this was not discoverable through the documentation index, homepage, or the (JS-rendered, non-scrapable) marketing pricing page within this retrieval exercise.
